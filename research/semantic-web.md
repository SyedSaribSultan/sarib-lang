# RQ2 — Why the Semantic Web stalled while schema.org, JSON-LD, and property graphs spread

Raw research notes for the .sarib language project. Each bullet is a claim with an inline source.
Weakly-sourced or unverified claims are flagged inline as **[WEAK]** or **[UNVERIFIED]**.
Research date: 2026-07-14. Method: web search + primary-source fetches (W3C specs, creator blog posts, insider retrospectives, academic papers).

## 1. The original vision (2001) vs. what shipped

- Berners-Lee, Hendler & Lassila published "The Semantic Web" in Scientific American, May 2001, promising a web "meaningful to software programs," where autonomous agents would, e.g., negotiate a mother's physical-therapy appointments by reading appointment data "supplied by the agents of individual providers through their Web sites." [Source](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/the-semantic-web/) (full scenario quoted in [Two-Bit History](https://twobithistory.org/2018/05/27/semantic-web.html))
- The plan: annotate pages with RDF metadata; RDF Schema + OWL would define ontologies enabling machine inference; the W3C issued the foundational standards 2001–2005 (RDF REC 2004, OWL 2004, SPARQL later). [Source](https://twobithistory.org/2018/05/27/semantic-web.html)
- By 2006, Berners-Lee's own co-authored retrospective ("The Semantic Web Revisited," IEEE Intelligent Systems, with Shadbolt & Hall) admitted: "This simple idea, however, remains largely unrealized." [Source](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/262614/1/Semantic_Web_Revisted.pdf)
- Same 2006 paper, on RDF/XML: the example rendering "is actually quite clumsy syntactically, and its lack of transparency and readability might have been a factor inhibiting rapid adoption of RDF" — i.e., the founders themselves blamed the syntax. [Source](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/262614/1/Semantic_Web_Revisted.pdf)
- Same paper on real projects: typical Semantic Web projects harvest legacy data into "a single, large repository" with custom interfaces, but "in general, however, they lack real viral uptake... The data exposure revolution has not yet happened." [Source](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/262614/1/Semantic_Web_Revisted.pdf)
- At AAAI 2006, Google's Peter Norvig challenged Berners-Lee with three barriers: (1) incompetence — "many people have difficulty writing well-formed Web pages; how would they get RDF right?"; (2) commercial dominance — why would a market leader open its data?; (3) deception (spam/SEO). Berners-Lee replied people were not supposed to hand-craft RDF but generate it from databases with scripts. [Source](https://grandtextauto.soe.ucsc.edu/2006/07/18/googles-norvig-questions-berners-lee-on-the-semantic-web/)
- The blogger reporting that exchange (Nick Montfort) added the most telling data point himself: "Although Berners-Lee closed by encouraging everyone to contribute data that they had in RDF to the Semantic Web, here I am typing up a blog post instead." — prose beat data-entry even among sympathetic experts. [Source](https://grandtextauto.soe.ucsc.edu/2006/07/18/googles-norvig-questions-berners-lee-on-the-semantic-web/)
- Cory Doctorow's "Metacrap" (2001) pre-dismissed the metadata utopia as "a pipe-dream, founded on self-delusion, nerd hubris, and hysterically inflated market opportunities" (7 problems incl. people lie, people are lazy, people are stupid). [Source](https://people.well.com/user/doctorow/metacrap.htm) (context in [Two-Bit History](https://twobithistory.org/2018/05/27/semantic-web.html))
- Clay Shirky, "The Semantic Web, Syllogism, and Worldview" (Nov 2003): "The Semantic Web is a machine for creating syllogisms," and syllogisms "don't work well in the real world because most of the data we use is not amenable to such effortless recombination"; he classed it with "technologies that are actually political philosophy masquerading as code" (with Xanadu and Freenet). [Source](https://gwern.net/doc/ai/2003-11-07-clayshirky-thesemanticwebsyllogismandworldview.html)
- Aaron Swartz (posthumous *A Programmable Web*): the failure came from the "formalizing mindset of mathematics and the institutional structure of academics"; W3C standards emerged from "Talmudic debates" so abstract few were adopted, and XML-family specs were "uniformly scourges on the planet... that have pushed out sensible formats (like JSON)." [Source](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/3/3f/Aaron_Swartz_s_A_Programmable_Web_An_Unfinished_Work.pdf) (quoted in [Two-Bit History](https://twobithistory.org/2018/05/27/semantic-web.html))
- Sean B. Palmer's periodization (public-lod list, 2017): phase 1 standards golden age (2001–2005), phase 2 Linked Data / DBpedia (2006–), phase 3 pragmatic adaptation (JSON-LD, schema.org), phase 4 W3C "Data Activity" retrenchment. [Source](http://lists.w3.org/Archives/Public/public-lod/2017Oct/0003.html) (via [Two-Bit History](https://twobithistory.org/2018/05/27/semantic-web.html))
- Academic insider review: Pascal Hitzler, "A Review of the Semantic Web Field," CACM 64(2), 2021 — traces "triumphs and challenges" of two decades; the field survives mainly as knowledge-graph research. [Source](https://cacm.acm.org/research/a-review-of-the-semantic-web-field/)
- Aidan Hogan, "The Semantic Web: Two Decades On" — balanced insider assessment of what survived. [Source](https://aidanhogan.com/docs/semantic-web-now.pdf)

## 2. Developer-experience critiques of RDF

Primary insider source: Manu Sporny (creator/lead editor of JSON-LD 1.0), "JSON-LD and Why I Hate the Semantic Web," Jan 2014. [Source](http://manu.sporny.org/2014/json-ld-origins-2/)

- Triple granularity / missing data structures: "RDF is a shitty data model. It doesn't have native support for lists. LISTS for fuck's sake! The key data structure that's used by almost every programmer on this planet." [Source](http://manu.sporny.org/2014/json-ld-origins-2/)
- Blank nodes: "Blank nodes are an abomination that we need, but they are applied inconsistently in the RDF data model (you can use them in some places, but not others)." [Source](http://manu.sporny.org/2014/json-ld-origins-2/)
- Toolchain weight: "to work with RDF you typically needed a quad store, a SPARQL engine, and some hefty libraries. Your standard web developer has no interest in that toolchain." [Source](http://manu.sporny.org/2014/json-ld-origins-2/)
- The devastating dogfooding admission: "after 7+ years of being involved with Semantic Web / Linked Data, our company has never had a need for a quad store, RDF/XML, N3, NTriples, TURTLE, or SPARQL. When you chair standards groups that kick out 'Semantic Web' standards, but even your company can't stomach the technologies involved, something is wrong." [Source](http://manu.sporny.org/2014/json-ld-origins-2/)
- Reification pain: standard RDF reification requires four to five additional triples per annotated statement ("effectively quadrupling your dataset size") and is hard to maintain in SPARQL; this verbosity is the acknowledged motivation for RDF-star. [Source](https://www.ontotext.com/knowledgehub/fundamentals/what-is-rdf-star/), [Source](https://blog.metaphacts.com/citation-needed-provenance-with-rdf-star)
- The RDF-star WG itself frames the lack of statement-level metadata ("edge properties" in property-graph terms) as a leading reason users choose labeled property graphs over RDF (Ora Lassila, RDF-star WG list, Apr 2024). [Source](https://lists.w3.org/Archives/Public/public-rdf-star-wg/2024Apr/0019.html)
- IRI/prefix ceremony: Ruben Verborgh & Miel Vander Sande (Semantic Web journal, 2020): "the prefix expansion for the OWL ontology counts 30 characters... FOAF is forever stuck at version 0.9, the Dublin Core vocabulary at 3 different ones... our public SPARQL endpoints are down 1.5 days a month." RDF and OWL "are sometimes labeled as 'by academics, for academics' because of their perceived complexity by developers." [Source](https://ruben.verborgh.org/articles/the-semantic-web-identity-crisis/)
- Verborgh & Vander Sande also concede: "the Web is a good platform for data publication, [but] a pretty bad platform for data consumption," and "most researchers in our community have not built a single Semantic Web app" — the field shipped publishing formats without consumer applications. [Source](https://ruben.verborgh.org/articles/the-semantic-web-identity-crisis/)
- Published RDF in the wild was full of syntactic and ontological errors ("Weaving the Pedantic Web," Hogan et al., LDOW 2010 — the canonical error survey; cited by Verborgh as evidence you cannot assume valid RDF). **[WEAK — paper cited via Verborgh's references, not read this session]** [Source](http://ceur-ws.org/Vol-628/ldow2010_paper04.pdf)
- Counterpoint (Dan Brickley, schema.org lead, commenting on Sporny's post): the RDF abstraction is why Google could swap Microdata-in-email to JSON-LD-in-email "without having to re-work any of the associated schema.org vocabularies... RDF's simple-minded classes and properties graph abstraction does find a niche." Abstract data model = format agility. [Source](http://manu.sporny.org/2014/json-ld-origins-2/)
- TerminusDB (Gavin Mendel-Gleason) postmortem on the syntax ladder N-Triples → prefixes → Turtle: better, but "these people [who say syntax is superfluous] are wrong and their opinions will drift into obscurity the same way that Turtle and XML will. Naming is deeply important... how much more important is sentence structure?" [Source](https://terminusdb.com/blog/the-semantic-web-is-dead/)

## 3. OWL: description-logic complexity vs. what users needed

- OWL's designers knew: within the OWL working group there was tension that "OWL DL was too complex to be understood by new users or implemented"; the underlying SHOIN(D) description logic has NExpTime-complete key inference problems and "is difficult to present to naive users" (Ian Horrocks, OWL's principal logician). [Source](https://www.cs.ox.ac.uk/people/ian.horrocks/Publications/download/2005/Horr05c.pdf)
- TerminusDB postmortem (author has written machine-checked proofs in Agda/Coq/FDR4): "The problem with OWL is that it solved a problem, but not one that anyone wanted to be solved. It also did it in a very hard-to-use way. So hard to use that almost nobody has successfully used it. I've tried... never experienced the same pain as with OWL." [Source](https://terminusdb.com/blog/the-semantic-web-is-dead/)
- Concrete failure modes (same source): declaring A subClassOf B and B subClassOf A doesn't error — OWL silently *equates* the classes ("equivocation by circularity is virtually never what one means. Yet I found numerous examples of these cycles in wild OWL"). [Source](https://terminusdb.com/blog/the-semantic-web-is-dead/)
- No Unique Name Assumption ("any two URIs might actually mean the same thing unless we explicitly say otherwise") + Open World Assumption together make basic constraint checking "stupidly complicated": "OWL would rather make two people the same, than complain that you've done something wrong." [Source](https://terminusdb.com/blog/the-semantic-web-is-dead/)
- Pat Hayes (a formalizer of RDF/OWL semantics), quoted in the same post: "Inconsistencies produce nonsense more than obvious contradictions... found one in Wikidata that implied that native Americans are insects, another that Paris is an 'abstract media type'." [Source](https://terminusdb.com/blog/the-semantic-web-is-dead/)
- Authoring ergonomics: "OWL makes you wander far from home when trying to define the meaning of a class" — properties, domains, ranges, restriction classes all defined as separate distant blocks; "I've seen people completely skip ontologising because the pain barrier was so high." [Source](https://terminusdb.com/blog/the-semantic-web-is-dead/)
- Inference pollution: OWL entailments materialize into the graph looking identical to source data — no provenance separation like a SQL view, so people resorted to batch reloads. [Source](https://terminusdb.com/blog/the-semantic-web-is-dead/)
- What users actually needed was validation, not inference: industry "got fairly fed up... and eventually invented SHACL," leaving "two different languages which must now be kept in sync" (OWL for semantics, SHACL for constraints). [Source](https://terminusdb.com/blog/the-semantic-web-is-dead/)
- Verborgh: in practice "ontologies are in practice often reduced to more prescriptive vocabularies that basically again leave semantics up to individual applications. The desire for more simple choices with less flexibility is illustrated by the backing of Schema.org... and the increasing popularity of the shape languages SHACL and ShEx." [Source](https://ruben.verborgh.org/articles/the-semantic-web-identity-crisis/)
- The founders' 2006 paper already conceded the authoring gap: "as yet we have few means to routinely and effortlessly generate Semantic Web annotations... at the point of content use or creation." [Source](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/262614/1/Semantic_Web_Revisted.pdf)

## 4. Why schema.org succeeded

- Launched June 2011 by Google, Bing (Microsoft), and Yahoo (Yandex joined later) as "a collaborative initiative... a standardized vocabulary for marking up web content." [Source](https://queue.acm.org/detail.cfm?id=2857276)
- Creators R.V. Guha (Google; earlier co-created RDF itself and RSS), Dan Brickley, Steve Macbeth — i.e., schema.org was built by semantic-web insiders who deliberately simplified. [Source](https://queue.acm.org/detail.cfm?id=2857276)
- Adoption numbers from the creators' own paper (ACM Queue, 2015): in a sample of 10 billion pages (Google index + Web Data Commons), 31.3% of pages had schema.org markup, up from 22% one year earlier; extrapolated, at least 12 million sites. [Source](https://queue.acm.org/detail.cfm?id=2857276)
- Stated success drivers (same paper): "the search engines and tools rallying behind it," plus "extensive support from third-party tools such as Drupal and WordPress extensions" and vertical CMSs (Bandsintown, Ticketmaster) — copy-paste and plugin-mediated adoption, not ontology engineering. [Source](https://queue.acm.org/detail.cfm?id=2857276)
- The incentive was direct and selfish: rich results in search. Google's own docs cite Nestlé measuring pages shown as rich results getting an 82% higher click-through rate. [Source](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/intro-structured-data)
- schema.org explicitly refuses the semantic web's grand ambition: its data-model page states it is not attempting a "universal ontology"; design is "a more practical and less abstract one, where immediate applications in search results were the focus." [Source](http://schema.org/docs/datamodel.html) (analysis in [Two-Bit History](https://twobithistory.org/2018/05/27/semantic-web.html))
- Mechanism insight: schema.org inverted the semantic web's economics — one giant consumer (search engines) guaranteed a payoff for every individual publisher, where the original vision required many consumers to exist before publishing paid off. (Synthesis of the above sources; see also Peter Mika, "What Happened to the Semantic Web?", ACM Hypertext 2017 keynote slides. [Source](https://ht.acm.org/ht2017/images/MikaPeterACM%20Hypertext%202017-WhatHappenedSemanticWeb.pdf))

## 5. JSON-LD: adoption reality (SEO snippets, not linked data)

- JSON-LD became a W3C Recommendation in January 2014; Sporny is explicit about its motive: "The desire for better Web APIs is what motivated the creation of JSON-LD, not the Semantic Web. The Semantic Web was near the bottom of my list of 'things to care about'." [Source](http://manu.sporny.org/2014/json-ld-origins-2/)
- Sporny deliberately erased RDF from the spec: "I made it a point to not mention RDF at all in the JSON-LD 1.0 specification because you didn't need to go off and read about it to understand what was going on." His summary of the strategy: "burning most of the Semantic Web technology stack (TURTLE/SPARQL/Quad Stores) to the ground and starting over." [Source](http://manu.sporny.org/2014/json-ld-origins-2/)
- Adoption is massive — as *embedded page metadata*: W3Techs (July 2026) measures JSON-LD on 54.5% of all websites; compare Open Graph (Facebook's RDFa-derived share metadata) 71.3%, Microdata 22.1%, while classic semantic-web-era formats are dead: Dublin Core 0.8%, Microformats 0.5%. [Source](https://w3techs.com/technologies/overview/structured_data)
- W3Techs classifies all of these under "structured data formats [that] allow search engines and other bots to extract specific data from web pages" — the category itself is SEO/social-preview metadata, not linked-data publishing. [Source](https://w3techs.com/technologies/overview/structured_data)
- Google's documentation drives the format choice: "Google recommends using JSON-LD for structured data if your site's setup allows it" (rich-results eligibility is the carrot). [Source](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/intro-structured-data)
- Two-Bit History's assessment of why JSON-LD and OpenGraph won: "you can use them without knowing anything about subject-predicate-object triplets, RDF, RDF Schema, ontologies, OWL... Nobody wants to use a tool that can only be fully understood by reading a whole family of specifications." [Source](https://twobithistory.org/2018/05/27/semantic-web.html)
- The "linked" part mostly isn't happening: Verborgh — "While the call for Linked Data has brought us the eggs, the chickens that were supposed to be hatching them are still missing, partly because making sense of others' data remains hard." Data is published; almost nothing consumes it as a graph beyond search/social preview parsers. [Source](https://ruben.verborgh.org/articles/the-semantic-web-identity-crisis/)
- Sporny on JSON-LD's own data-model section: "I assert that we could rip the data model section out of the JSON-LD specification and it wouldn't really affect the people using JSON-LD in any significant way" — even JSON-LD's users don't use its RDF semantics. [Source](http://manu.sporny.org/2014/json-ld-origins-2/)
- TerminusDB's verdict even on JSON-LD: "a very serious attempt to bring Linked Data into the JSON developments and should be applauded. It is, however, needlessly verbose as an interchange format." [Source](https://terminusdb.com/blog/the-semantic-web-is-dead/)
- **[INFERENCE, flagged]** The claim "JSON-LD in the wild ≈ schema.org snippets for search engines" is a synthesis: W3Techs measures embedded page markup; Google mandates schema.org vocabulary for rich results; no comparable non-SEO consumer exists at scale. No single survey directly measuring "fraction of JSON-LD that is SEO markup" was found this session.

## 6. Why property graphs (Neo4j) beat triple stores for developers

- Cypher's origin is visual, not logical: Neo4j engineer Tobias Lindaaker — "The design of Cypher was grounded in the diagrams I used to draw on whiteboards... [a colleague] would often transcribe those diagrams into ASCII art inside code comments... Cypher made those diagrams executable." [Source](https://www.thobe.org/work/cypher/)
- Cypher was built bottom-up from developer need in 2011 by Andrés Taylor *against management guidance* (Neo4j's founders opposed query languages); labels were added to the data model to make lookup-by-domain-value work. [Source](https://www.thobe.org/work/cypher/)
- The property-graph model matches "the way we draw a domain on the whiteboard," with properties directly on nodes *and relationships* — vs. RDF where annotating an edge requires reification; Neo4j's own comparison: "properties on predicates are clearer than RDF reification concepts which can be unfriendly to newcomers." [Source](https://neo4j.com/blog/knowledge-graph/rdf-vs-property-graphs-knowledge-graphs/), [Source](https://dzone.com/articles/rdf-triple-stores-vs-labeled-property-graphs-whats)
- The W3C side concedes the point: RDF-star exists because edge properties are "a feature similar to 'edge properties' in Labeled Property Graphs, the lack of which in RDF is often cited as the reason users choose LPGs." [Source](https://www.ontotext.com/knowledgehub/fundamentals/what-is-rdf-star/), [Source](https://lists.w3.org/Archives/Public/public-rdf-star-wg/2024Apr/0019.html)
- Market outcome: DB-Engines ranks graph DBMS (Neo4j consistently #1) as a mainstream category while tracking RDF stores as a separate, much smaller category (21 systems). [Source](https://db-engines.com/en/ranking/rdf+store) **[WEAK — relative popularity is visible in the rankings but no single number quoted here]**
- Formal follow-through: Cypher was opened as openCypher (Oct 2015) and evolved into GQL, an ISO/IEC standard database language alongside SQL (ISO/IEC 39075:2024). [Source](https://www.thobe.org/work/cypher/), [Source](https://www.iso.org/standard/76120.html) **[ISO number from prior knowledge, not re-verified this session]**
- Verborgh's parallel diagnosis for why simpler rivals win: GraphQL's success shows "substantially less powerful but far more developer-friendly technologies" beat the semantic stack; front-end developers are the gatekeepers of adoption. [Source](https://ruben.verborgh.org/articles/the-semantic-web-identity-crisis/)

## 7. Wikidata: the semantic web's actual success story

Primary source: Vrandečić, Pintscher & Krötzsch, "Wikidata: The Making Of," WWW 2023. [Source](https://iccl.inf.tu-dresden.de/w/images/9/9d/Vrandecic-Pintscher-Kroetzsch_Wikidata-History-WWW-2023.pdf)

- Lineage: Semantic MediaWiki (2005, Krötzsch/Vrandečić/Völkel at Karlsruhe) proposed "annotations in wikitext markup for embedding structured data into Wikipedia articles" — typed links inline in prose, the classic early-semantic-web "unity of data and text." [Source](https://iccl.inf.tu-dresden.de/w/images/9/9d/Vrandecic-Pintscher-Kroetzsch_Wikidata-History-WWW-2023.pdf)
- What real usage taught them (2005–2010): "form-based input methods soon dominated over in-text annotations – a major deviation from the unity of data and text that was central to the Semantic Wikipedia concept." [Source](https://iccl.inf.tu-dresden.de/w/images/9/9d/Vrandecic-Pintscher-Kroetzsch_Wikidata-History-WWW-2023.pdf)
- And: "it soon emerged that the provision of RDF-encoded data (fully conforming to Linked Data recommendations) did not lead to relevant applications. Instead, the ability to embed query results into wiki pages was what motivated users to add structured data." — users annotate for immediate in-wiki payoff, not for the linked-data commons. [Source](https://iccl.inf.tu-dresden.de/w/images/9/9d/Vrandecic-Pintscher-Kroetzsch_Wikidata-History-WWW-2023.pdf)
- Wikidata (launched late 2012) chose a "flexible, statement-centric data model – inspired by SMW, and in turn by RDF" but with qualifiers/references/ranks per statement, no fixed schema, and abstract Q-IDs (decoupling name from identifier, influenced by Metaweb/Freebase experience). [Source](https://iccl.inf.tu-dresden.de/w/images/9/9d/Vrandecic-Pintscher-Kroetzsch_Wikidata-History-WWW-2023.pdf)
- Wikidata is explicitly *not* OWL-based: "Wikidata is not based on a standard semantics such as OWL, although community modeling is strongly inspired by some of the expressive features developed for ontologies" — modeling norms are communicated via docs and shared query patterns, constraints via community tooling, not description logic. [Source](https://iccl.inf.tu-dresden.de/w/images/9/9d/Vrandecic-Pintscher-Kroetzsch_Wikidata-History-WWW-2023.pdf)
- Epistemology: the CACM 2014 design paper (Vrandečić & Krötzsch) codifies "plurality" — Wikidata records possibly conflicting claims with references and preferred/deprecated ranks rather than enforcing one truth. [Source](https://iccl.inf.tu-dresden.de/w/images/8/89/Wikidata-CACM-2014.pdf)
- Google closed Freebase in 2016 and helped migrate its data to Wikidata — the commercial knowledge-graph pioneer folded into the community project. [Source](https://iccl.inf.tu-dresden.de/w/images/9/9d/Vrandecic-Pintscher-Kroetzsch_Wikidata-History-WWW-2023.pdf)
- Why it worked where the semantic web didn't: proximity to Wikipedia gave it social + infrastructure support (Malyshev et al., ISWC 2018); a form/UI-based editing model (no syntax to learn); immediate consumers (Wikipedia infoboxes, then the SPARQL query service). [Source](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-030-00668-6_23)
- Semantic-web tech is the *export interface*, not the medium: WDQS is a Blazegraph SPARQL endpoint over an RDF-ized dump; ironically "the growth of Wikidata has outpaced the development of Open Source triplestores" — success strained the very stack it validated. [Source](https://iccl.inf.tu-dresden.de/w/images/9/9d/Vrandecic-Pintscher-Kroetzsch_Wikidata-History-WWW-2023.pdf)

## Design lessons for .sarib

1. **Adoption follows immediate, selfish, per-document payoff — never ecosystem promises.** schema.org won on rich-result CTR (+82% per Google/Nestlé); SMW users only added annotations once inline *queries* returned value into their own pages, while pure RDF export "did not lead to relevant applications." A .sarib file must do something useful for its author (agent answers, transclusion, checks) the moment a fact is written, with zero other adopters in the world. [Queue](https://queue.acm.org/detail.cfm?id=2857276), [Wikidata Making Of](https://iccl.inf.tu-dresden.de/w/images/9/9d/Vrandecic-Pintscher-Kroetzsch_Wikidata-History-WWW-2023.pdf)

2. **Syntax is destiny; meet authors inside a format they already write.** Berners-Lee's own 2006 paper blames RDF/XML's "clumsy" syntax for inhibiting adoption; JSON-LD won by being plain JSON; Sporny's spec deliberately never mentions RDF. .sarib should read as an extension of prose/Markdown habits, not as a new statement calculus. [Shadbolt/Hall/TBL 2006](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/262614/1/Semantic_Web_Revisted.pdf), [Sporny](http://manu.sporny.org/2014/json-ld-origins-2/)

3. **Model what people mean: lists, edge annotations, and provenance must be first-class, not encodings.** RDF's missing lists, inconsistent blank nodes, and 4-5-triple reification are the three most-cited wounds; Wikidata's qualifiers/references/ranks and LPG edge properties are what users actually reach for. Statements-about-statements belong in the core model of .sarib from day one. [Sporny](http://manu.sporny.org/2014/json-ld-origins-2/), [Ontotext RDF-star](https://www.ontotext.com/knowledgehub/fundamentals/what-is-rdf-star/), [Wikidata CACM](https://iccl.inf.tu-dresden.de/w/images/8/89/Wikidata-CACM-2014.pdf)

4. **Default to closed-world validation with unique names; make open-world inference an explicit, segregated opt-in.** OWL's open world + no unique names made it unable to tell an author "you made a mistake" — the single most practical failure (industry built SHACL to escape it, and inferred triples polluted data indistinguishably). .sarib should check documents like a type system and keep derived facts visibly separate from asserted ones. [TerminusDB](https://terminusdb.com/blog/the-semantic-web-is-dead/)

5. **Names for humans, identifiers for machines — and let tooling do the mapping.** IRI ceremony (30-char prefixes, dereferencing rituals, breakable endpoints) taxed every RDF author; Wikidata succeeded by hiding stable abstract IDs (Q42) behind human labels in the UI. .sarib should let authors write plain names locally, with durable IDs assigned/resolved by the system. [Verborgh](https://ruben.verborgh.org/articles/the-semantic-web-identity-crisis/), [Wikidata Making Of](https://iccl.inf.tu-dresden.de/w/images/9/9d/Vrandecic-Pintscher-Kroetzsch_Wikidata-History-WWW-2023.pdf)

6. **Record claims, not truth.** Doctorow's metacrap objections (people lie, disagree, err) sank the one-true-metadata web; Wikidata's plurality model (conflicting referenced claims with ranks) is the design that survived contact with reality — and it maps naturally to an AI-native medium where the reader (an LLM) can weigh sources. [Metacrap](https://people.well.com/user/doctorow/metacrap.htm), [Wikidata CACM](https://iccl.inf.tu-dresden.de/w/images/8/89/Wikidata-CACM-2014.pdf)

7. **One guaranteed consumer bootstraps the format.** The semantic web had publishers with no consumers ("eggs but no chickens"); schema.org had Google, Wikidata had Wikipedia infoboxes. For .sarib the guaranteed consumer is the AI agent reading the user's own files — design every feature so the agent visibly uses it, closing the incentive loop without any network effect. [Verborgh](https://ruben.verborgh.org/articles/the-semantic-web-identity-crisis/), [Two-Bit History](https://twobithistory.org/2018/05/27/semantic-web.html)

8. **Spec readability is a product feature.** Sporny attributes JSON-LD's success partly to a spec written for web developers ("most specification editors are just crap at writing"); Swartz blamed "Talmudic" standards for the semantic web's stall. The .sarib spec should teach by example in its first 70%, conformance language later. [Sporny](http://manu.sporny.org/2014/json-ld-origins-2/), [Swartz via Two-Bit History](https://twobithistory.org/2018/05/27/semantic-web.html)
